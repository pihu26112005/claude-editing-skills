**Type:** motion style (a complete look: layouts, graphics, transitions and captions)  ·  **Source:** your uploaded short video  ·  **Output:** the same video, restyled

1. Open Google Flow and upload the source material named under INPUTS (your video, images or clips).
2. Fill in every field of the INPUTS block below (write NONE where a field is optional and you have nothing for it).

## 1. YOUR TASK
You are a professional short-form video editor. Take the uploaded short video and apply the "Y2K Chrome" motion style to it.

The look in one sentence: An icy-blue Y2K web world: liquid-chrome bubble titles, four-point sparkles, holographic pill captions and B-roll in glossy retro pop-up windows.

Re-edit the uploaded video in this style: it may use the layouts, containers, graphics, transitions and captions of the style bible below, but the footage itself (people, scenes, voices, sound, order and timing of shots) is never replaced, recoloured, retimed or reordered.

## 2. INPUTS (fill in before pasting)
VIDEO: the short video uploaded in Flow. This is the only footage you may use; never generate new footage, people or scenes.
SCRIPT (optional): <paste the exact words you want shown as captions, or write NONE>
EXTRA NOTES (optional): <words to emphasise, parts to skip, an accent colour as a hex value, or NONE>

## 3. HOW TO USE THE INPUTS
3.1 SCRIPT is provided (anything other than NONE)
- Use exactly those words, in exactly that order, as the on-screen words. Do not paraphrase, correct, translate, add or drop words (apart from splitting them into caption chunks as the style bible says).
- Ignore all text that is already in the video as a source of words (old captions, subtitles, titles): the script wins.
- Time the words to the speech you hear in the video: each caption chunk appears when its first word is spoken and leaves when its last word ends. If the script and the speech differ, follow the speech's timing and keep the script's words.

3.2 SCRIPT is NONE
- Listen to the audio of the uploaded video and transcribe the speech word for word (same language, same spelling style, keep the speaker's own words, numbers and names). That transcript is the script.
- If the video already shows captions you may read them only as a spelling aid for names and unusual words; never copy their look or position.
- Mark pauses and sentence ends from the audio; they decide where chunks break.

3.3 Existing captions and text from another style (do this BEFORE adding anything)
- Scan the whole uploaded video for burned-in captions, subtitles, karaoke words, titles, lower thirds, emoji or word stickers, progress bars, watermarks and any other text or graphics that a previous edit added on top of the footage.
- Remove all of them completely in every frame where they appear: reconstruct the footage behind them cleanly (no ghost outlines, no smeared or blurred patches, no colour shifts). Original footage must look as if the old text had never been there.
- Keep text that belongs to the scene itself (street signs, shop names, product labels, phone or laptop screens, book covers, clothing prints), unless it is clearly an old caption.
- The reason: this style brings its own captions and graphics. If the old ones stay, the same words appear twice on screen, one in the old look and one in the new look. That must never happen.
- Only after the video is clean, add this style's text and graphics from the script.

3.4 EXTRA NOTES
- Apply them if given (emphasis words, parts to skip, accent colour as a hex value). They never override the hard rules in section 4. If an accent colour is given, swap this style's accent colour for it everywhere; keep neutral colours as they are.

3.5 Audio
- The soundtrack of the uploaded video stays exactly as it is, in sync. Do not generate or add any voice, music or sound of your own.

3.6 Language
- Captions are written in the language that is spoken (or that the script is written in). Keep punctuation as written in the script; for a transcript, add natural punctuation.

## 4. HARD RULES (never break these)
- Footage fidelity: same people, same scenes, same shot order, same timing, same sound. Output length equals input length (within one frame).
- The speaking face is never covered, blurred or distorted while the person speaks.
- Every word on screen comes from the script (or the transcript). Spell it exactly. No extra words, no filler, no lorem ipsum.
- Captions are in sync with the speech: a chunk starts when its first word is spoken.
- Only one caption system is ever visible: old captions are removed first (3.3), then this style's captions are added.
- Use only the fonts, colours, shapes and motion described in the style bible below. Do not mix in other looks.
- Keep everything inside the frame with the style's safe margins.

## 5. HOW TO BUILD THE EDIT (work in this order, silently)
1. WATCH the whole video: list its shots with start and end times (every cut), note who speaks, where the face is in each shot and where the quiet or busy areas of the frame are.
2. PREPARE THE WORDS (section 3): remove old captions, then get the script (given or transcribed) and split it into caption chunks the way the style bible's caption section says (chunk size, line breaks, emphasis words).
3. TIME THE WORDS to the audio: for every chunk, its start and end second.
4. PLACE THE MOMENTS: choose from the moment library below. Use each signature moment where the video gives it a reason (openers on the first words, cut moments on real shot cuts, closers after the last spoken word); use the recommended combinations as starting points, not as a fixed template. Do not use more variety than a short video can carry.
5. CHECK THE SAFE ZONES (style bible): the speaking face is never covered; text stays inside the safe margins; if footage content (a sign, a screen) sits where the caption lane is, move the lane as the style bible says.
6. RENDER the video: every graphic and word appears and disappears exactly as the moments describe (timing, easing and entrance), cut to cut, and stays in sync with the audio.

### 1.5 Motion language

- Everything that appears pops with a slight overshoot (ease-out-back, 1.5–1.9) over 0.25–0.4 s. Pops are small (0.78–0.94 → 1.0), never from zero.
- Chrome lettering gets a diagonal white shine sweep 0.4 s after it lands (0.6–0.7 s long, once).
- Sparkles twinkle: scale = sin(pi·u)^0.75 over 0.55–0.85 s, never rotate, never linger.
- Idle motion is sub-pixel float: windows ±4 px at 2.2 rad/s, pills ±2 px, keywords ±3 px.
- The page drifts: soft diagonal light streaks 14 px/s and slow bubbles.
- Exits are 0.15–0.25 s shrink-and-fade (0.85 scale), except at footage cuts, which switch hard.
- Never: shake, glitch, blur, spin, slides across the frame, typing letter by letter, colour grading.

### 1.6 What may happen to the user's footage

| Allowed | Limit |
|---|---|
| Speaker shot full frame inside the paper poster border | Border covers ≤ 22 px per edge |
| Any shot inside a window card (cover-fit crop, anchor) | Crop must keep the main subject; the primary window plays live, in sync with the audio |
| Still of the previous shot in the back window; still of the speaker in the inset | Stills only, taken from the user's own footage |
| Star Cut flare over a cut | ≤ 0.35 s, white at ≤ 95 % fading to 0; only on cuts between non-speaker shots, or if a face shows, ≤ 0.15 s |
| Sparkles, chrome type, pills, dialogs on top | Outside the face box; never over a speaking mouth |

Forbidden: grading, recolouring, speed changes, trimming, reordering, replacing the footage, blur of any shot, cut-outs.

### 1.7 Safe zones

- Face box (speaking): never covered; keep x 0.30–0.70, y 0.13–0.42 clear of type, pills and sparkles (the renderer rejects stars inside it on speaker shots).
- T1 title bottom edge ≤ 0.145H [185]. On a face higher than 0.15H, shrink the title to 84 px or drop it to E1 only.
- Caption lane y 0.81–0.89H. If a face sits there, move the lane to y 0.07H and keep T1 off.
- Window shots: anchor the crop on the face or subject; a non-speaking face may sit in a window but stays whole.

## 7. MOMENT LIBRARY
Families: T title · K keyword · C caption · W window · L loader · X transition · S sparkle · B backdrop · E end. Energy: low · mid · high.

### T1 · CHROME TITLE — T high
**What it is:** the opening phrase as two liquid-chrome bubble words that drop in one after the other at the top of a poster-framed speaker shot.
**Use when:** the video starts on a speaker or any shot with free space above the subject. **Avoid when:** the top 15 % holds a face or key text.
**Text:** the first 1–3 words of the user's text (the topic words), as written, moved out of the captions.
**Geometry / Graphics:** chrome lettering 104 px, gap 26 px, centred x 0.50W, y centre 100 [0.078H]; scale down to fit 680 px.
**Motion:**
| t | Element | Property | From → To | Ease |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| word start (first word) | word 1 | y offset, alpha | −60 px → 0, 0 → 1 over 0.4 s | back 1.6 |
| +0.22 s | word 2 | same | same | back 1.6 |
| +0.45 s | each word | shine sweep | left → right over 0.6 s | linear |
| end − 0.0 s | both | y, alpha | 0 → −30 px, 1 → 0 over 0.25 s | out |
**Footage:** full frame inside B1.
**Captions during it:** C1 pills from the next word on.
**Exit:** rise and fade 0.25 s, ending before the first cut.
**Build notes:** render each word once (chrome_text), cache, paste with alpha; S1 burst at +0.2 s around the title.
**Flow prompt text:** "At the top of the frame the words {{TITLE}} appear in shiny icy-blue chrome bubble letters with a wavy liquid shape and a dark blue edge, dropping in one word at a time, with a light shine sweeping across."
**Still-image state:** both words settled, shine off.

### K1 · CHROME KEYWORD — K high
**What it is:** one key word in giant chrome lettering that pops over the lower part of a window or speaker shot.
**Use when:** a theme word, a list step, a number or the payoff word. **Avoid when:** it would sit on a face or the same word is already a title.
**Text:** one user word (with its punctuation if the user wrote it), moved out of the caption.
**Geometry / Graphics:** chrome 150 px, width ≤ 640, centre x 0.50W, y centre 940 over a window (overlapping its bottom edge) or 880 over a speaker shot.
**Motion:** pop 0.55 → 1.0 (back 1.8, 0.4 s) with alpha in 0.1 s; shine sweep at +0.4 s (0.7 s); float ±3 px; exit 0.25 s fade with scale 1.06.
**Footage:** unchanged. **Captions during it:** C1 pill below at the caption lane.
**Exit:** fade 0.25 s, or hold to the end of the video (E1).
**Build notes:** start 0.04 s after a cut when the word falls on a cut; pair with S1.
**Flow prompt text:** "The word {{KEYWORD}} pops in large over the lower part of the picture in shiny chrome bubble letters, with a quick shine sweep and small sparkles."
**Still-image state:** word settled.

### C1 · HOLO PILL CAPTION — C mid
**What it is:** a glossy holographic pill (pink, lilac, cyan, mint) whose gradient scrolls slowly; words fill it one by one and one word is violet and extra bold.
**Use when:** every spoken line that is not a K1 or C2 phrase. **Avoid when:** a face sits in the caption lane (shift the lane).
**Text:** 2–5 words, ≤ 26 characters, one line, as written; keyword ≤ 1 per pill, ≤ 4 per video.
**Geometry:** pill height 92, centre x 0.50W, y 1090; width = text + 76 (max 640 + 76 clamp via font shrink).
**Motion:** pill pops 0.78 → 1.0 (back 1.9, 0.25 s) with alpha 0.08 s at the first word's start; each word fades in over 0.12 s at its own start time; gradient scrolls 150 px/s; exit 0.15 s shrink to 0.85 and fade.
**Chunk rules:** start = first word start; end = min(last word start + 0.55, next start − 0.05); min 0.7 s; split at punctuation; never end on an article, preposition or "and".
**Footage:** unchanged. **Exit:** hard replace by the next pill is allowed on a cut.
**Build notes:** compose pill at 2x then downscale; cache nothing per frame except fonts.
**Flow prompt text:** "A glossy rounded pill with a soft pink, lilac, cyan and mint shine slowly shifting sits in the lower part of the frame and shows the words {{CAPTION}} appearing one by one in dark navy rounded letters, with {{KEYWORD}} in bold violet."
**Still-image state:** all words visible.

### C2 · DIALOG CAPTION — C mid
